Repair one memory. Keep the rest.

I connected three learning memories across six NPUs on the BrainChip AKD1500.
The first recognizes a signal; the next two learn how to map it to an output.

Then I changed the signals. The output dropped to 16/64 correct.

With teaching enabled only for recognition, the chip learned the new signals
using four additional existing neuron records. Every word of the original four
learned records stayed unchanged. The two downstream memories stayed unchanged too.

Across three initializations: 64/64 new signals and 64/64 old signals after repair.
The frozen controls stayed at 16/64 on the new signals.

This is still a small synthetic task. The host supplies inputs and teaching
labels; the chip selects the connections it changes. The video is recorded
hardware data, with reconstructed responses and a schematic layout.

Tools and verification: https://github.com/MidasMulli/akd1500-lab
